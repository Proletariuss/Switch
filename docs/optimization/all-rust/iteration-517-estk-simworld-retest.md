# Iteration 517: SIM World real-card IMS path retest

2026-10-18. This is a hardware checkpoint, not SIM World business acceptance. A separate current-source `switchd-rs` ran under `build/runtime/iteration-517-simworld*`; the installed app and its data were not changed. The ESTKme-RED PC/SC reader connected and reported the active SIM World 454/03 subscription. No profile switch, download, SMS send, or call was performed.

Two baseline starts completed IKE/EAP authentication and installed the tunnel, then ended with `startup_failure={stage:ims_transport,code:transport_failed,io_operation:plain_connect,io_kind:timed_out}`. The public status briefly showed `tunnel_state=connected, ims_state=registering`, then `tunnel_state=error, ims_state=failed`; that intermediate tunnel status is **not** IMS readiness. Surge's effective profile selected Enhanced Mode over `utun` because its SOCKS5 UDP ASSOCIATE probe was unavailable. Its active request accounting showed outbound UDP traffic and IKE replies, followed by outbound traffic without further inbound bytes during IMS TCP SYN retries. A previous run used the Hong Kong 13 residential policy, while this retest used the Hong Kong 15 residential policy; both timed out. The Surge request list continued to label the UDP flow by its original port 500, which does not by itself prove that later datagrams missed port 4500.

To test whether the kernel's connected UDP socket hid the NAT-T destination transition from the proxy, a temporary development change used `send_to`/`recv_from` on an unconnected socket while preserving its local port. The real-card result was unchanged: IKE tunnel established, IMS plain TCP timed out, and the same Surge flow accounting appeared. **That experiment was reverted.** The development binary was rebuilt from the restored source, the focused transport test passed, and both isolated sessions were disabled and shut down cleanly. No DMG was generated.

This evidence does not distinguish an ESP packet rejected by the ePDG from a proxy forwarding problem or a wrong inner destination. The next hardware check needs bounded, non-secret evidence for the negotiated ESP suite, actual post-transition UDP destination, ingress ESP count, and selected P-CSCF candidate. Avoid changing carrier/proxy rules or claiming SMS/call readiness without that evidence. Previous Iteration 515 evidence already found zero inbound ESP packets during the initial IMS attempt.
